## 1. Tower of God — high-level craft

### Opening promise

Official English hook is a desire inventory, not a lore dump: “What do you desire? Money and wealth? Honor and pride? Authority and power? Revenge? Or something that transcends them all? Whatever you desire—it’s here.” (S1). Genre is labeled Fantasy. The first listed episode is explicitly **\[Season 1\] Ep. 0**, then **Ep. 1 - 1F.Headon's Floor** (S2): the serial announces a closed-space test structure immediately.

SIU’s own origin image, reported in earlier interviews and restated in the 2025 WEBTOON conversation, is hierarchical space: a closed world that stratifies vertically, with some beings trying to break layers and others trying to keep them (S5). He says the work is a **world-first** construction: setting and structure precede characters, after which characters begin to lead (S3, S5).

**Transferable craft, not plot:** open with a personal want attached to a spatial machine the reader can picture in one beat.

### Hook speed

Ep. 0 exists as a named prologue before Floor 1 testing (S2). SIU emphasizes that Korean webcomics hear reader feedback after one or two episodes rather than after a collected volume (S3). Tapas’s platform guidance independently prefers a first episode that can be understood in ~5 minutes while still hooking (S17). Exact scroll-second timing of Ep. 0’s first reveal is **`not_assessed`** (image-native viewer; panels not downloaded).

### Protagonist appeal / silhouette

Official copy does not sell a power fantasy first; it sells desire plus entry into an unfair machine (S1). SIU later contrasts Bam with Urek Mazino as “direct opposite personality” while both can carry a story (S3). High-level silhouette craft: the lead is readable as **want + mismatch with the tower**, not as finished iconography. Fine facial construction and early-vs-late body proportions are **`not_assessed`**.

### Supporting-cast differentiation

SIU states that battle-story “positions” connect to football roles and that the tower is multicultural (S3, S4). The transferable principle is **verb-separated team functions** (direct attack, long-range, recon, information relay, flow control) rather than five similar pretty fighters. Named position labels, weapons, and faction costumes are **prohibited similarity**.

### Emotional stakes

SIU’s long-run craft goal is “not boring the readers” after the first freshness fades (S3). Emotional engine is conflicting private desires inside a shared climb, not a single damage-number. Exact panel acting (tears, micro-expression) is **`not_assessed`**.

### Mystery / future-vector

The official question “whatever you desire” plus floor numbering (1F Headon) creates an open vector: more floors, more tests, unanswered want (S1–S2). Season 3 still uses rift/battle episode titles years later (S1). The craft is **durable unanswered want**, not a one-volume boss kill.

### Progression visibility

Progression is spatial and social: floors, tests, team positions, later ranker/irregular status in the public encyclopedia (S6). It is not a numeric HUD in the official English landing copy. Visible rank-as-geography is the transferable idea.

### Antagonist iconography

Public floor-guardian naming (Headon in Ep. 1 title) and later “Great Families / King” structure in reference summaries (S6) show **named, role-legible opposition**. Specific creature designs, horns, and color keys are **`not_assessed` / prohibited to copy**.

### Quiet-to-spectacle

SIU’s “keep it going without boredom” (S3) implies rotation of test type, not constant climax. CLIP STUDIO’s webtoon paneling guide independently recommends rhythm/contrast and even balloon-only beats (S18). Exact quiet-panel ratio in ToG is **`not_assessed`**.

### Vertical reveal timing / shot-size / depth / line / anatomy / color / effects / balloons

WEBTOON’s native format is continuous vertical scroll for mobile (S1; SIU on web-first Korean comics, S3). Variety’s WEBTOON executives describe the format as a continuous vertical digital canvas (context, 2025). **Panel-level** shot-size variation, value grouping, line confidence, hands, costume continuity, color-script, effects hierarchy, attack/counter beats, and balloon/SFX geometry are **`not_assessed`**: licensed pages are image strips; this review did not download them.

**Production evolution (officially visible):** SIU trained in visual arts education, drafted in military service, serialized from June 2010, and later used assistants (S6). 2025 interviews treat the series as a 15-year craft object whose market and readers changed (S3). Early-vs-late line density is widely discussed in fandom but **not independently measured here**.

## 2. Solo Leveling — high-level craft

### Opening promise

Tappytoon: weakest hunter of mankind; party wipe in an overpowered dungeon; a System chooses him as its sole player; he can level beyond known limits (S7). Yen Press Vol. 1 adds concrete adult stakes before the spectacle: mother’s hospital bills, sister’s tuition, no job prospects, so he keeps taking raids (S9). Publisher genres: Action and Adventure, Drama, Fantasy, Video Game Related (S9). The promise is **baseline humiliation + visible rule that can change a decision**.

### Hook speed

Vol. 1 contents in the licensed look-inside listing: Prologue → Double Dungeon → Three Commandments → Quest (S9). The first collected unit is already a raid-failure plus rule-reveal, not worldbuilding travel. Exact first-screen timing on KakaoPage/Tappytoon is **`not_assessed`**. KakaoPage’s series home did not return readable copy in a JS-blocked fetch (S11); serialization on KakaoPage from 2018-03-04 is treated as a catalog fact, not a viewed episode.

### Protagonist appeal / silhouette

Official nickname is an anti-silhouette: “Weakest Hunter of All Mankind,” E-rank (S9). Growth is the later silhouette change. Transferable craft: **start with a body/status the reader can pity and track**, then let costume/posture/value change become the scoreboard. Copying the later dark-coat / shadow-army silhouette is prohibited.

### Supporting-cast differentiation

Vol. 1 copy already separates a missing-arm party leader from a “quivering” healer (S9). REDICE/ORV interviews (same studio family) stress psychological distinction and pose/expression direction (S12). Transferable: **one job, one fear, one body cost per ally**, not a chorus of identical hunters.

### Emotional stakes

Yen Press binds raids to family bills and lack of alternatives (S9). That is adult economic coercion, not abstract “save the world” yet. Le Monde’s public portion frames the comic as a phenomenon whose studio still talks staging, structure, and drawing quality after the original artist’s death (S10).

### Mystery / future-vector

Tappytoon: secrets deep within dungeons and the ultimate extent of his powers (S7). The System is a mystery-as-interface: it both explains and withholds. Transferable: **the rule that helps the hero is also the unanswered plot**.

### Progression visibility

“Sole player” + “level up his abilities, possibly beyond any known limits” (S7). Yen Press classifies the comic as video-game-related (S8–S9). Transferable: **the reader must see the increment apply in the next fight**, not only in a menu. Copying blue holographic windows, hunter ranks, gates, or shadow soldiers is prohibited.

### Antagonist iconography

Vol. 1: “a being whose power outranks anything he’s ever seen” (S9). VFX Voice’s licensed anime-adjacent reporting notes storyboards that emphasize beast size versus human hunters (S19). Transferable: **scale and rank-as-shape**, not a specific statue design.

### Quiet-to-spectacle

The published opening is failure, bills, then dungeon impossibility (S9). Spectacle is earned by establishing incompetence. Later weekly chapters (Tappytoon “Every Mon”, S7) must keep a baseline/payoff rhythm or the level-up loop numbs. Exact later-arc quiet ratio is **`not_assessed`**.

### Attack / counter / reversal

Cartoon Brew: the anime action director studied live-action fight flow because the protagonist’s combat is “brutal-yet-amateuristic,” and urban environments become part of the scene (S20). That is adaptation craft, not the webtoon’s panel geometry, but it confirms the source’s readable fight *intent*: weight, amateurism, environment as weapon. Panel-by-panel reversal grammar in the manhwa is **`not_assessed`**.

### Color-script / effects / line / balloons / UI

Yen Press markets “full-color glory” and credits a dedicated letterer, Abigail Blackman (S8–S9). REDICE is a studio, not a lone weekly cartoonist (S10, S12). Disciples, taking over after DUBU, told Le Monde the teacher was strict about **mise en scène, structure, and overall drawing quality**, and that the successor would keep that standard (S10). Transferable production craft: **storyboard → drawing → color → QC**, with lighting/pose direction unified by an art lead (S12). Specific blue System chrome, glow recipes, and SFX fonts are **`not_assessed` and prohibited**.

### Production evolution (officially visible)

Webtoon 2018–2021 under DUBU(REDICE); epilogue/side stories under DISCIPLES(REDICE) after 2022 (S7, S8, S10). Le Monde: the assistant hired around tome 16 had to continue within weeks; the pen name “Disciples” names that apprenticeship (S10). This is one of the few licensed cases where **late-run quality is an explicit succession problem**, not just “the artist got better.”

## 6. Causal gap (why this is below the named bar)

The primaries’ transferable bar is not “more rendering.” It is:

1. **A first-screen want or humiliation the reader can repeat.**
2. **A spatial or rule machine that changes a decision inside the opening unit.**
3. **Role-legible bodies, not interchangeable illustration beauty.**
4. **Variable rhythm: quiet acting, then one spectacle that spends setup.**
5. **Lettering and UI as directing, not a band glued under a key visual.**
6. **A storyboard/QC pass that owns pose, contact, and last-panel propulsion.**

Our pipelines already write several of those as bibles (Ember’s eight-beat fights, Borrowed Down’s load thesis, City’s covenant costs). The rasters still behave like **prompted illustration sheets**: equal-cell or equal-height plates, painterly convergence, caption rectangles, and chapter-end tableaus. That is why schema PASS and density percentages do not produce ToG/SL-class sequential reading.

unOrdinary / Weak Hero are the warning: if we cannot yet hold REDICE finish, the competitive path is **acting + impact + weekly question**, not more texture.

## 7. Original improvement directions (non-imitative)

- Author **variable-height scroll units** (full-bleed scale, close acting, balloon-only breath) instead of 1:1 generated plates.
- Put the opening want **in the picture**: a body that cannot yet do the job, not only a pretty void.
- Treat supporting cast as **verbs in silhouette** (brace, read, call, sever) without ToG position names.
- Show progression on **wear, posture, and one accent**, never a copied System window.
- Budget **episode-end pictures** the way Sleepy-C budgets them: the last beat is a directing meeting, not leftover overlay copy.
- Keep Borrowed Down’s tactile contour logic as a house option; do not “upgrade” it into Solo Leveling gloss.
- Letter inside reserved negative space **designed in the storyboard**, not baked as a cream box or a 76% opaque caption card.

Prohibited globally: tower-climb tests, irregulars, shinsu, hunter gates, blue HUD, shadow army, reincarnated king, mana core, subway scenarios, named-artist style prompts, or any uploaded third-party panel.
