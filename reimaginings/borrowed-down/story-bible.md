# Borrowed Down — story bible

## Spoiler-light premise

At the edge of the vertical Serein Sea, the city of Veyr rents gravity by the hour. When the evening weight bells fail, municipal ropewright **Mae Nox**, 41, and blacklisted forecast smuggler **Dax Pell**, 38, must carry a stolen anchor-heart through districts turning sideways. Every force they survive can be braided into one-use craft—but using it transfers the consequence somewhere else.

## Story thesis

No structure is neutral: somebody carries its load. Progress means learning not merely how to redirect force, but who has been made to absorb it.

## World

The Serein Sea rises like a continent-high blue wall along Veyr's eastern edge, held upright by ancient stone ribs. Twice daily, its gravitational lean migrates through the city. Civic anchor engines distribute "down" through chains, street keels, and public weight bells. The ruling Plumb Council quietly diverts stability toward the Crown terraces, leaving labor districts to suffer slippage.

The wall-sea contains immense, non-malicious current beasts. **Keelbacks** swim through its vertical depth; juvenile **glass skates** leak through failing ribs; debris-hunting **knot crows** steal charged cord. The supposed monster crisis is largely a consequence of civic extraction.

## Progression: burdencraft

Saltglass fibers remember a force that their bearer physically survives while making a conscious choice. A ropewright can braid the memory into a visible knot and later release it once: pull, brace, recoil, breath, heat, buoyancy, or shared load. Nothing is created. Released force transfers to an anchored substrate, damages the cord, and leaves a colored pressure scar on the bearer or equipment. Larger craft requires consent and synchronized tension among multiple bearers.

Ranks are practical, not numerical:

1. **Witness** — identify a stored force by touch.
2. **Binder** — store one personally survived force.
3. **Rigger** — route two forces through tools and terrain.
4. **Caller** — coordinate a shared load among consenting people.
5. **Span** — become one node in a civic-scale living structure without owning it.

## Principal adults

### Mae Nox, 41 — municipal ropewright

Tall, broad-shouldered, dark brown skin, black hair in one blunt wedge braid, squared jaw, ochre work coat, teal harness, heavy split-toe boots. She braces before she speaks. Goal: keep South Hook standing and earn authority to repair the system correctly. Flaw: confuses procedure with fairness and takes every load herself. Speech: exact nouns, dry understatement. Danger style: calculates leverage, then commits bodily. Arc: custodian → dissident → coordinator who trusts distributed agency.

### Dax Pell, 38 — blacklisted forecast smuggler

Lean, copper-brown skin, asymmetrical silver-black curl crest, narrow moustache, coral storm cape cut short on the right, charcoal wrap trousers, yellow barometer gauntlet. Always tests air with two fingers. Goal: expose the Council forecast that killed his former crew and clear their names. Flaw: weaponizes charm and withholds information. Speech: metaphors, jokes under pressure, sudden blunt truth. Danger style: improvises with motion and social reads. Arc: evasive loner → reliable witness → public truth-teller who accepts shared responsibility.

### Orra Venn, 56 — Plumb Council engineer

Compact, shaved head, white triangular mantle over black load armor, brass plumb-staff. Mae's former mentor. Believes sacrificing peripheral districts prevents total collapse. Her love for the city is real; her arithmetic denies personhood.

### Tavi Rusk, 47 — South Hook cook and union caller

Round, powerful adult silhouette, indigo head wrap, sleeveless boiler apron over practical clothes, carries a cleaver only as a tool. Blunt, communal, funny. Evolves from neighborhood organizer to the first civilian Caller.

### Eshe Pell, 44 — Dax's older sister and Crown archivist

Long angular face, copper-brown skin, black bob cut perfectly horizontal, slate archive coat with yellow seam marks. She kept evidence safe by appearing loyal and resents Dax for abandoning the slow work.

## Relationship engine

Mae initially sees Dax as reckless evidence contamination; Dax sees Mae as the Council's favorite pulley. Attraction appears as attention to competence, then care during recovery, then anger when withheld truths remove consent. They do not become a frictionless couple. By Chapter 10 they choose a deliberate partnership and an honest first kiss after the crisis, while preserving conflicting civic ambitions.
