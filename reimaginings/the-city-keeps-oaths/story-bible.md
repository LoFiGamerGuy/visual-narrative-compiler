# The City Keeps Oaths — story bible

## Core promise

Adult pathwright Sola Merrow discovers that Caelune's luminous roads are living records of completed civic promises. When Magistrate Serac Dain uses a hidden mill to burn those promises twice, Sola must move from solitary expert to coordinator of a city whose people retain ownership of their own commitments.

The opening volume asks: what holds a city together—one powerful protector, or bounded obligations ordinary people can actually keep?

## World

Caelune is an original city of inhabited pearl-stone crescents suspended above a blue cloud sea. Sparse cyan-gold roads bridge the open air. Architecture is clean, civic, and readable: broad terraces, ivory ribs, midnight-glass apertures, and occasional living light. No tower, dungeon, guild-rank ladder, game interface, or borrowed faction system exists.

Roads are made from oathglass, a responsive mineral that records voluntary promises witnessed, costed, and completed. Government offices have gradually replaced reciprocal covenants with vague obedience oaths. The resulting maintenance deficit is concealed by the Oath Mill, which extracts energy from already completed commitments.

## Principal fictional adults

- Sola Merrow, 38: municipal pathwright. Wants to repair every failure herself; needs to accept distributed competence and an identity beyond usefulness. Non-negotiable: never knowingly leave a traversable route unsafe. Practical, non-sexualized design.
- Tarin Kest, 45: senior warden. Wants lawful continuity; needs to choose public duty over command and replace authority with service. Non-negotiable: never abandon a person inside a hazard perimeter.
- Kesin Or, 52: archivist, nonbinary. Wants a complete public record; needs to risk losing unique evidence so living people can act. Non-negotiable: never falsify a source.
- Serac Dain, 51: continuity magistrate. Wants Caelune aloft at any cost; needs to recognize that an unbounded sincere vow can be tyrannical. He remains consequential because his core promise is true.
- Commander Varo Pell, 48: command officer. Wants order and institutional survival; her debt after Sola saves her life creates the lawful mechanism that ends Serac's emergency rule.

All are visibly mature fictional adults. There are no children, young-looking protagonists, real-person likenesses, sexualized designs, or private references.

## Relationship arc

Sola initially treats Tarin as an access credential; Tarin treats Sola as a risky expert. Mutual witnessed choices change this into candid partnership. Kesin begins as a neutral recorder and becomes a participant who sacrifices irreplaceable evidence. Serac functions as Sola's negative mirror: both believe competence confers obligation, but only Sola learns to bound and distribute it.

## Volume resolution

CH10 restores the founding compact as a plural, revocable covenant. Caelune stops falling through thousands of bounded acts rather than a single victory blow. Serac is deposed and detained under restored law. Outer Lantern survivors are found and rescue begins. The next-volume vector is outward: long-abandoned roads beyond Caelune answer Sola's new Chorus.
