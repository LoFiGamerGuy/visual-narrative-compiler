# Quiet surfaces, forceful combat

This round extends the approved source-preserving texture process into sequential combat. It is a provisional production method, not a cast or canon decision. Existing Black Petal and texture-refinement guides remain unchanged and available in their original deliveries.

## Build a fight around changing options

Write a short state sequence before generating: intention → contact → consequence → response. A power needs a visible source, a specific target, a readable result and a limitation that another combatant can exploit. The next image should inherit the result rather than reset it. Escalation can come from losing footing, range, cover or time; it does not require a larger glowing effect.

Keep a compact state record for each moment: positions, weapon ownership and handedness, planted or lifted implements, active spell area, injuries, damaged clothing, altered terrain and remaining escape routes. Establish the geography before close action. A tiny figure can retain a distinctive hair, coat, weapon and posture silhouette, but a name in a caption is not evidence of recognition.

Use no more than five actual image references in one built-in request. Give every reference a narrow purpose: identity, location, actual preceding state or a contact diagram. More references do not guarantee consistency. Inspect their relevance and remove redundant inputs before freezing a request. Geometry guides are schematics, not proof that the final anatomy works.

## Inspect contacts before finishing

Trace each weapon continuously from its owner’s hands to its working end. Check that a shield is held from behind, a blade touches the intended target, a planted butt meets the ground, and both feet can bear the depicted load. Follow the physical response through the whole body. A bright impact ring can hide a creature that has not actually recoiled.

Compare adjacent actual images without captions. Ask what visibly changed and why. If the source already contradicts a state, record that failure. A future brief may reconcile an actual position, but this must not rewrite the earlier failure as compliance. Do not call a blocked exit a completed escape.

## Apply the established surface redraw selectively

Use one exact source image for a finishing pass. Preserve camera, composition, faces, bodies, weapon construction, grip, scale, lighting and action. Replace repetitive microtexture with broad opaque material shapes and selective crisp accents. Preserve material differences and inhabited depth. Do not blur, denoise, flatten the world or add ornament to compensate.

Protect **causal detail**, not every fleck: the water moving with a force vector, the gap between severed horn ends, the contact under a loaded boot, a visible wound, and the changing edge of an ice route. These details explain the action. Simplifying them can make a calmer image narratively weaker.

Compare the returned finish against its exact source at native size and the same phone width. A cleaner image is not automatically an improvement in every dimension. D05’s finish reduces distracting surface activity but also weakens visible continuing-force spray; that tradeoff remains recorded. Surface finishing cannot silently fix geography, weapon ownership, anatomy or spell-state failures.

## Preserve evidence and review independently

Freeze prompts, actual reference hashes and plan version before calls. Retain every returned native image and every no-art failure. Separate structural repairs from texture passes. The W06 six-reference rejection is preserved as a failed invocation; its narrowly corrected five-reference resubmission uses an existing retry slot and is never described as an unchanged transport retry.

Read each completed fight with captions off, then inspect individual art, history and native zoom. Keep drawing, impact, power, causality, recognition, texture, setting and character preferences independent. Leave owner choices blank. Production selection of a calmer attempt is not owner approval of the design.
