# Vesper Reach — The Cost of a Counter

This delivery studies longer combat in three separate sequences: a twelve-moment duel, a six-moment coordinated group fight and a six-moment siege. They share a provisional world and recurring adult designs, but are not one chronological episode or owner-selected canon.

Open `../../docs/combat-depth/index.html` to read. The reader starts with the duel and captions off. Use Read group or Read war for the other studies. Gallery and Browse expose independent ratings, notes, shortlist, comparisons, related subjects, native zoom and full generation history. Save/load exports or validates and imports this round’s choices. Previous exported choices remain read-only and separate. Fresh choices are blank.

## What this tests

The duel develops an exchange rather than a collection of isolated attacks: approach, contact, damage, forced movement, escape, anticipated counter and close pressure. The group fight tests complementary combat roles. The siege tests whether powers can shape a collective retreat and leave a costly change in the world.

The approved quiet-surface method continues through separate source-preserving redraws. Precise faces, loaded stances, weapon contacts and a few consequential effects carry impact. All underlying primaries, repairs and finishing attempts remain available for comparison; the displayed attempt is a production choice, not a user vote.

## Evidence and limitations

Read `COMBAT-WORKFLOW.md` for the reusable process and `MAGIC-AND-COUNTERS.md` for provisional abilities, limitations and progression possibilities. The independent-review directory preserves native judgments before root discussion, phone addenda and sequence findings. Root observations are separate. These checks document what is visible; they do not prove that the owner likes a design.

Some spells lose required contact or active-state cues between moments. Some damage markers drift, close binds can confuse weapon ownership, and intended distant framing sometimes becomes foreground hero framing. A quieter finish can also weaken an action cue: D05 retains the pain and wall damage but loses some force-direction spray. The final review notes identify remaining image-specific weaknesses rather than treating a repair as automatically successful.
