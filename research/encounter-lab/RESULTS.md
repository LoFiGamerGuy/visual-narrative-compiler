# What the encounter test shows

The clearest improvement is the space after impact. Faces, posture, damaged equipment and distance between opponents make a fight feel as though it happened to someone. Soft Thunder's duck and shared laughter, Nightglass's broken guard and separated recovery, and Black Petal's looming claw and fallen mask are useful examples. The five drawing treatments remain visibly different.

The comparison board suggests a practical lesson: strong action changes a body's balance, an opponent's options or the next composition. More particles do not supply that missing consequence. This round tests that lesson through linked sequences and extreme framing, rather than copying published characters or panels. Source titles and uncertainty are documented in the comparison board; the search screenshot is not treated as a set of individually approved references.

Sixteen selected source-preserving finishes reduce pervasive scratching, crack networks, rust flecks and floor grain. The method is still useful across multiple styles. It is not infallible: NG03-F1 weakened a protected shoulder burst to a small glint, and X03-F1 erased visible hammer-face damage. Their earlier versions remain the defaults and both finishes remain inspectable. X01-F1 also smooths a chipped guard edge that was already in a doubtful injury location; its cleaner display has that qualified damage tradeoff. NG04-F1 is quieter but its floor appears more polished and reflective. Some broad floor mottling and localized scorch texture remain elsewhere.

The sequences communicate the broad exchange more reliably than their exact power rules. Nightglass's haft-to-shoulder transfer remains ambiguous. Black Petal does not clearly demonstrate the initially ineffective body strike, and its weapon hand and creature supports drift. Iron Orchard depicts front collapse rather than the planned flank roll. Soft Thunder's arm mark changes sides. Sunbreak's flame path branches or separates instead of forming the intended single-point return. These are actual visible limitations, not problems solved by captions or cleaner surfaces.

The opening beats often start at contact; separate anticipation and feinting need more room. Four images can suggest an encounter but do not yet demonstrate a lengthy duel. Radical alternatives provide stronger proximity or scale, while sometimes changing contact, grip or damage details. They are labeled alternate experiments, not inserted as canon continuation panels.

The next useful decisions are which encounters make you care about what happens next, which individual faces and implements remain appealing across views, and which powers you can understand with captions hidden. Keep those answers independent. Use the comparison board to identify whether the missing ingredient is deformation, timing, framing or a visible consequence.
