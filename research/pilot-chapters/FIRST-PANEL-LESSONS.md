# From attractive moments to readable chapters

The pilot method is artwork-informed, then story-led. Existing images establish what is appealing about a direction; a complete short script establishes decisions, consequences and panel order. New panels must serve those beats. Existing library artwork stays available as an option, never silently becomes canon or a finished comic panel.

The first six story panels exposed concrete input conflicts. A cast sheet describes stable identity, but its equipment prose also described possession: a wallet on a belt, a bell on a horn, a coat over an arm. Repeating that prose after a handoff or rescue contradicted the current story. Later unsubmitted inputs now separate stable construction from the exact current holder, location, damage and power state. Original inputs remain preserved as unsubmitted revisions; changing an input before a call is not another image generation.

Drawing treatment also needs separate scope. A style clause that praised Floodline's umbrella creature was still a positive creature instruction during a clue shot where that creature had not been revealed. The revised medium clauses describe brushwork, planes, palette and line treatment without requesting an absent subject. Explicit visible-cast and crop constraints govern the picture.

The first FL clue misread a still-water 'pocket' as a clothing pocket. The correction names a horizontal ground puddle bounded by stone. Concrete spatial language is more useful here than compact terminology. This observation identifies an ambiguity in our submitted input; it does not establish how the model internally processed it.

Lettering is tested on actual output. Two attractive two-shots reversed left/right speaker placement. Source-bound layout uses the exact frozen speaker labels where clean tails cannot convey the intended order, and later inputs propose actor blocking compatible with reading order. Dialogue stays editable outside generation. A new repaired source gets a fresh layout check: the NG09 repair added a tether through its previous balloon location.

Source-only texture finishing remains separate from structural repair. NG01 and NG02 retain city depth and acting with calmer surface marks. A finish must not be credited with fixing hand ownership, a tether endpoint or a power-rule inconsistency. Those limitations stay visible in the reviews even when an edited image is the better display option.

Next production practice: start with a short complete chapter; freeze exact dialogue; define stable cast construction; track state per panel; test a few wide/dialogue/contact/clue panels; correct unsubmitted inputs; draw the complete sequence; inspect native art and the lettered phone chapter; finish only surfaces that compete; preserve all attempts and blank owner preferences.

The first complete sequences also show the limit of text state tracking. Nightglass keeps the rescue choice and its cost, while several panels move the tether away from the pommel or reverse the sword hand. Red Current keeps the broad counterattack, but does not consistently display the stored charge, arm state or earned key. These are distributed continuity failures: correcting one isolated frame would not establish the full rule. A full chapter can be readable while its mechanics still need work.

For the next production experiment, test an adjacent pair or short action chain with the selected preceding panel and a simple current-state drawing as explicit references. Keep the original style anchor available, but distinguish style, stable identity and changing story state in reference roles. Compare that route against the present cast-sheet route before expanding it. This is a recommended unrun test, not a demonstrated improvement in this delivery.

Do not repair a rendering failure by changing the script after generation. For example, an animal offered as a traveling partner should not silently become a harnessed cart animal merely because a departure image draws it that way. Repair the picture where a bounded correction is justified, or disclose the mismatch. Keep the exact planned dialogue and intent available alongside actual output.
