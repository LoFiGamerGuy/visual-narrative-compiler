# Artwork suggests the series; story directs the chapter

Use a hybrid. The original anchor establishes the kind of world we want to enter, the appeal of its people, its creatures and its drawing language. Develop a provisional premise from those strengths. Then write a small complete chapter with a want, pressure, a meaningful choice, a changed situation and a next question. Generate new compositions for those specific beats.

Writing around a collection of unrelated attractive images risks weak transitions and arbitrary events. Writing a large script without testing its visual identity risks losing the appeal that made the world worth following. Here the five scripts precede their new panels, while the preserved art remains a reference and an independent future option.

## Five independent paths

Nightglass leads with elegant character appeal and an inhabited vertical city. Black Petal preserves intimate portrait horror and translucent floral threats. Soft Thunder uses broad painted shapes, physical comedy and expressive creature partnership. Red Current keeps sharp nib drawing, black/white masses and red combat accents. Floodline Detective keeps coarse brushwork, mature faces, flooded architecture and one pink accent. They are not one shared universe by default.

All nine original directions and every anchor-return attempt remain in the option library. The four paths without a pilot in this round remain available. A provisional story or cast assignment does not erase a prior alternative or fill an owner rating.

## Production order

1. Inspect original anchors and the actual previous artwork. Identify what belongs to the medium and what was merely noise or an earlier failed experiment.
2. Draft and independently review sixteen distinct story beats per pilot. Resolve motivation, geography, causal gaps and exact copy before spending artwork generations.
3. Freeze the plan, visible cast, current physical state, camera, quiet lettering space and bounded attempt allowance. Future damage must not leak into earlier panels.
4. Generate a compact new cast sheet for each path. Inspect actual faces, proportions, hand ownership and equipment before using it as a reference. A sheet is assistance, not proof of continuity.
5. Generate new unlettered panels using the original drawing benchmark and the inspected cast reference. Treat close acting, tactile clues, distant geography, action and aftermath as different jobs.
6. Review actual native art. Use structural repairs only for observed failures. Keep every attempt and record what each repair did and did not fix.
7. Apply source-only surface redraws only where texture competes with the story. Preserve the source's medium, faces, contact, damage and material. An already quiet panel needs no finish.
8. Place editable dialogue and speech tails against the selected image. Bind any placement override to that image's SHA; keep the frozen words unchanged. Inspect the actual phone chapter, including speaker attribution, reveal order and protected faces/hands.
9. Keep art appeal, story comprehension, technical checks and owner preferences separate. A working reader does not prove an exciting comic, and a successful impact does not prove an entire power rule.

## What to decide after reading

Choose independently which protagonist you want to follow, which world invites another chapter, which drawing treatment feels right, and which story made you curious. Prefer precise scene or dialogue reactions over mechanically combining every liked component. The next refinement should target the strongest specific path while preserving the other paths for their own development.
