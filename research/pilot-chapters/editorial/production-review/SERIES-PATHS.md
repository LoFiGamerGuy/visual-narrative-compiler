# Five provisional series paths

These are possible continuations of the five completed pilots, not selected canon. Each world stands independently. The owner can prefer a drawing treatment, character, relationship or power without accepting the rest of its package. The questions suggest possibilities, not new scripts.

## Nightglass Courier — The Delivery Below

Aren wants access to the city’s upper routes, yet his choice to rescue Pell costs him qualification. Advancement offers freedom; the people he encounters complicate what he will do for it. His frustration after the rescue matters as much as his competence.

The recurring engine could be difficult routes whose apparent delivery task exposes a larger city problem. The pilot already joins immense vertical architecture, a terrifying glass creature, a limited pulling line and a personal letter from Lower District. Discoveries should arise through traversal, decisions and combat.

**Next-chapter question:** Who addressed this letter to Aren, and why does its Lower District origin trouble the people above?

**Still undecided:** Aren as the continuing lead, Sera’s future role, the sender’s identity, and whether the route system should become a formal progression hierarchy.

## Black Petal — A Voice That Knows Your Name

Ilyra can reject a false voice without giving up hope for Oren. Her personal tension is the difference between evidence worth following and intimacy she desperately wants to believe. Neris supplies both living stakes and a sharper, practical counterpoint.

The recurring engine could follow tangible clues through elegant places made dangerous by cultivated life and borrowed voices. The world promises beautiful, threatening glasshouses and creatures with observable behavior; their rules should enable deductions and risky choices.

**Next-chapter question:** What does the nursery know about the button from Oren’s coat?

**Still undecided:** Oren’s fate, the nursery’s responsibility, the nature of imitation, and whether Neris becomes a recurring companion.

## Soft Thunder — No Ticket for Thunder

Tavi approaches trouble with confidence and power, then earns a better outcome by noticing what another creature needs. Her tension is between performing competence and admitting she can learn. The negotiated ride should remain a partnership, not quietly become ownership.

The recurring engine could be travel problems solved through action, observation and imperfect deals. Mara’s skyport invitation and the floating island promise an inviting world. Grounded pulse limits can grow through timing and control while the ram remains a character with preferences.

**Next-chapter question:** What does their first skyport require from a traveler arriving with an independently minded ram?

**Still undecided:** A permanent traveling group, the ram’s name and longer-term abilities, Mara’s role, and how far the series leans toward comedy versus peril.

## Red Current — One Hit Owed

Ren wins the key and discovers that victory has created debt. His tension is ambition against the consequences of treating warnings as somebody else’s problem. Vexa’s concern strengthens the rivalry.

The recurring engine could connect earned access, dangerous opponents and increasingly consequential uses of borrowed force. The ascending rings and enormous presence below promise progression in both scale and obligation. Victories should stay real even when their costs change what winning means.

**Next-chapter question:** What does the earned key open while Ren’s spent arm remains unreliable?

**Still undecided:** The lender’s identity and motives, repayment terms, Vexa’s allegiance, and the wider structure of advancement.

## Floodline Detective — The Coat That Walked Home

Corin’s experience is valuable, but his first assumption is wrong. His tension is professional authority against the need to listen—and his past connection to the institution now implicated.

The recurring engine could be supernatural cases in which material clues, limited water-memory and dangerous practical encounters expose concealed responsibility. Ada’s active rescue and Lio’s testimony keep the case human. Monumental floodworks promise a city whose infrastructure and mysteries are inseparable.

**Next-chapter question:** What was the night shift told about the supposedly empty sluice?

**Still undecided:** Who within Flood Authority is responsible, how much Corin previously knew, the siblings’ future involvement, and the eventual scope of his investigative powers.
