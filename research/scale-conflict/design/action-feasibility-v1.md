# Independent action feasibility: The City That Moves at Dawn

Frozen proposal before generated results or lead preferences. The recurring cast and city are provisional exploration subjects, not canon. This note uses the lead’s task brief; the latest substantive owner message was not present verbatim in this agent’s context. No artwork or selection state was changed.

The premise has a strong visible engine: people inhabit enormous districts that change position at dawn, while something below pushes against their foundations. Its main risk is producing twenty-four attractive people facing impressive backgrounds. Give every conflict a contested route, a visible deadline or a cost transferred to somebody else. Huge scale needs human infrastructure: occupied balconies, one market canopy, a bridge queue, small doors and a usable street. Thousands of window dots are unnecessary.

## Four mechanic proposals

**1. Riven — Borrowed Distance.** Keep spatial seam movement and the same saber. A saber tip touching an existing cyan seam lets him step to one visible connected seam exit; he cannot invent an exit or cut through a body remotely. Show one body at the destination and the just-vacated entry clearly empty, rather than two Riven copies. A hostile moving district can carry the exit out of alignment. Growth moves from crossing one exposed gap, to carrying one other person, to choosing among several visible exits under pressure; longer reach does not remove the need for a stable entry and exit. Combat demonstration: an opponent’s heavy strike commits to the entry platform; Riven emerges beside its exposed flank with blade still in hand, while the strike damages the empty original position. Long-term tension: maps expire at dawn, and control of safe seam junctions becomes military and political power.

**2. Neris — The Unspent Blow.** Keep pressure capture and the crescent shield. A braced outer shield face catches one directional pressure front. One clean compressed arc against its face shows stored load; no particle halo. She must release it into a real target or grounded support before taking another full impact. Growth increases how much she can hold and how precisely she can return it, without creating unlimited storage. Demonstration: the front of a broad blast ends against the shield, cloth behind her falls quiet, and a later outward discharge knocks one attacking foreleg off its footing. The rear grip remains on her side; the outer face meets incoming force. Long-term tension: she can save a crowd now but must choose where the captured force goes. That choice can conflict with Riven’s urgent escape plan.

**3. Kellan — Winter Lock.** Keep contact-water freezing and the straight sword. The edge touches an existing wet join; a single continuous ice wedge grows from that point and temporarily arrests the join. Dry air and distant stone do not freeze. Growth moves from stopping a weapon sliding through water, to bracing a bridge joint, to holding one heavy moving mechanism long enough for a counterattack. Demonstration: blade touches the water-filled gap between two approaching stone masses; a blue-white wedge occupies the gap, their edges remain separated, and water is displaced to either side. He must maintain contact against load, leaving himself exposed. Combat use: bind a charging creature’s planted foot to a wet surface, then exploit the halted charge. Long-term cost: holding one moving city joint transfers strain elsewhere; frost cannot become a consequence-free city pause button.

**4. New rival — The Still Crown.** An optional power direction, not a frozen identity. One planted scepter establishes a local still point: one contiguous district or large structure ceases moving relative to it, while adjacent districts continue. The effect ends when the scepter loses contact; boundaries are one decisive seam, not a field of runes. Early growth anchors a shield-sized stone, later a platform, ultimately a district at a severe personal and civic cost. Demonstration: the rival stands erect on one calm terrace while its connecting bridge visibly shears away toward a moving tower. This is both regal combat control and the conflict’s moral edge: holding a hospital district can endanger a poorer district downstream. Keep the rival capable of a defensible choice, rather than automatically evil. Do not introduce free gravity reversal, time stopping and summoning alongside this one rule.

These four powers complement one another but should not require all four to solve every encounter. Each needs a solo success, a visible failure condition and an occasion when another person’s solution makes theirs harder.

## Recognizable silhouettes and framing

- Riven: silver hair mass, cropped petrol jacket and one continuous saber. Use a low forward diagonal, a wary glance or a visibly interrupted step; preserve the reference’s face and clothing instead of adding cloak or armor to signal importance.
- Neris: dark loose knot, pale shoulder forms, long teal torso shape and unmistakable crescent. Open the gap between forearm and torso enough to trace arm–cuff–grip. Use grounded, protective body language with a readable person or route behind her.
- Kellan: black curls, sand cropped jacket with cobalt inset, straight sword with both hands on its actual hilt when loaded. His compact bent stance should contrast with Neris’s broad brace and Riven’s long diagonal.
- Rival option: an adult with an angular face, blunt dark jaw-length hair, one broad pale collar and a long restrained mulberry silhouette, holding one straight scepter with a single open-circle head. A still upright pose supplies regal contrast without jewelry noise. Develop the actual identity after the first generated evidence; this is not a fourth locked cast sheet.

Across twenty-four images, a useful test allocation is four extreme wides with lead figure height roughly 3–8% of the canvas, six wides at 10–22%, eight medium scenes at 25–45%, and six close actions where the controlling hand/face/weapon can exceed 50% and deliberate body crops are allowed. These are staging targets, not automatic quality scores. Do not enlarge foreground heroes in extreme wides just to preserve facial recognition. Let location, costume masses and subsequent medium shots carry identity. The four linked moments count inside these twenty-four.

Vary vantage: street-level compression under a moving arch; a high oblique view exposing a bridge separation; a lateral medium with a clear incoming force; a close three-quarter hand/weapon contact; a distant view across daylight water. Avoid filling every frame with cyan night and a half-frame hero. Warm dawn, overcast pale stone, dark undercity and wet twilight can preserve the same material language.

## A feasible four-moment encounter

1. Wide: a district starts to slide; one connecting footbridge pulls away, exposing a widening gap. A crowd is visibly confined on the near landing, with Riven and Neris at the edge. Establish one pale tower and one dark arch as landmarks.
2. Medium: Neris intercepts a single pressure front from beneath the gap; the people behind her occupy a visibly quiet lee. Show the load direction, the outer shield face and her actual rear grip. The bridge remains separated.
3. Close action: Kellan touches the wet moving join with his sword and forms one ice wedge. Show the wedge actually between the two edges, with his hands on the hilt and the sword connected to the water. The joint holds; the original bridge is still broken.
4. Wide resolution with cost: Riven has crossed to the stable side with one rescued person while Neris releases her stored front into an empty buttress. The buttress breaks and falls into the exposed void; the crowd’s route is now usable, but a valuable lower passage is permanently lost. Keep the same two landmarks and motion direction. The rescue is a small win against a city-scale threat, not the complete solution to it.

This is one optional sequence. A source–action–target–consequence chain may need two images; a single frame should not pretend to prove an invisible before/after. For complicated power beats, establish the loaded state and show one decisive changed state next.

## Surface and review discipline

Use large charcoal and pale stone masses, sparse cyan structural seams, broad water reflections and a few meaningful windows. Keep doors, paths, inhabitants and load-bearing edges: removing those would damage scale and story. Faces, grips and contact edges deserve local precision; paving and distant façades do not need equal detail.

Follow the texture-refinement skill: first establish an appealing composition, then use a separate source-only surface pass only when the actual image is too busy. Preserve its identities, materials, contacts, depth and framing. Structural repair is separate; a calmer image can preserve a wrong grip. No automatic pass on already calm art, no added generation allowance, and no inference of owner acceptance from successful file checks.
