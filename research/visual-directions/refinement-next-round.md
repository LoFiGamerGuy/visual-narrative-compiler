## Review rubric

| Question | How to use the answer |
| --- | --- |
| Does the owner want to follow this character? | Record character preference separately from drawing treatment. |
| Is the focal face/pose/monster easy to read at ordinary phone size? | Review the full image without zoom first, then native detail. |
| Is background detail subordinate to the focal subject? | Look for broad quiet regions, distinguishable shapes and a useful hierarchy of edges. |
| Does texture or ornament serve a specific form? | Reject repeated decorative noise, muddy detail and conspicuous synthetic-looking artifacts. |
| Is it comfortable for the owner to look at? | Explicit discomfort is a rejection, regardless of an AI reviewer's score. |
| Does the actual image retain the intended drawing treatment? | Report drift rather than naming a style from its prompt alone. |

The owner liked detailed Cathedral Engine and Black Petal. Therefore the working interpretation is controlled, legible detail, not an automatic ban on detail or painted rendering. This is an inference from the selections, to be checked against the next owner review.

Collect independent answers for character appeal, rendering appeal, readability and visual comfort. Leave room for 'keep this part, reject that part' and short reasons. Do not collapse all answers into a weighted beauty score. Ask the owner to narrow to two or three directions after viewing the controlled set.

Then make a short sequence in the finalists using the same cast and scene: dialogue, a strong expression change, a full-body action pose and character/creature interaction. Face/costume continuity, intentional staging and comfortable sequential reading are the next questions. Additional unrelated concept galleries will not answer those questions.
