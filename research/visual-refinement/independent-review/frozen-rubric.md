## Separate questions, without a beauty score

| Dimension | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- |
| Character appeal and acting | Reviewer reaction supported by face shape, adult presence, expression and pose; whether retained identity survives. Keep “I find this engaging” separate from inferred owner preference. |
| Drawing treatment | Actual contour, shadow, material and texture construction compared with the selected reference. Labels describe intent until the pixels support them. Rendering appeal is its own observation. |
| Phone readability | Full image at ordinary 390px reader width before zoom: focal face, emotion, body silhouette, prop use and creature/subject relationship. Describe what needs enlargement and what remains ambiguous. |
| Visual comfort | Observable competing edges, high-frequency detail, contrast noise, quiet areas and hierarchy. Reviewer comfort is only a reviewer reaction; the owner's comfort is unknown until reported. Detail alone is not failure. |
| Technical integrity and comparison validity | Anatomy, hands, prop connections, object counts, accidental text/marks, identity/staging drift and consistency between inset and scene if applicable. Distinguish clear defect, uncertain relationship and intentional stylization. |

## Controlled-comparison confounds

- Retain Velvet's adult male identity: long delicate oval face, narrow nose, wine-red tied hair, slim dancer build and identifying costume/rapier features as frozen by the lead. Retain Tidewalker's adult female identity: broad mature brown face, broad nose/full lips, thick dark braid and visibly heavy powerful body. A more conventionally slim or youthful redesign is not proof of better rendering.
- Full original character references also contain rejected worlds/rendering. Watch specifically for 04's filigree, tangled foliage and wash texture or 16's all-over broken paint/coral detail leaking into every treatment. Retaining character ideas does not retain those rejected treatments.
- Hold pose, expression, camera, crop, subject/creature size, costume topology, prop ownership, creature design and world objects within each shared-character set. Log deviations visibly even when attractive. Style simplification can change edge treatment; it should not silently change the depicted event.
- Freeze palette/lighting policy explicitly. Palette shifts may be legitimate treatment choices but then conclusions concern the combined visual treatment, not isolated line or paint technique. Larger/brighter faces, fewer objects or a different smile can independently improve a result.
- Read the two characters as repeated tests of each style. A style that works only for the elegant male has not demonstrated equal transfer to the mature heavier woman. Two scenes do not establish sequence continuity or repeatability.
- Keep exploratory retained 11 and 14 combinations outside the 18-image controlled comparison. Preserve 11's older stocky compassionate character and 14's slender adult lavender-haired character without importing rejected treatments.

Inspect all 20 actual outputs in the phone reader at 390px without zoom, then native image detail; record file/candidate identity, visible evidence, uncertainties and any introduced artifacts. Where delivery order forces native-first inspection, disclose that order and still perform a separate full-phone pass. After all output and reader evidence is available, advise two or three **provisional technical finalists** on demonstrated identity retention, readable acting, controlled detail and transfer across both shared characters. Describe tradeoffs, unresolved confounds and evidence that could reverse the advice. Do not convert that advice into an owner ranking, comfort finding, acceptance, final cast choice or production-readiness claim. Owner approval stays null until explicit owner feedback exists.
