Her vest also has gathered creases and a center fastening that read as cloth rather than ceramic. These are visible reference biases: later outputs repeating them should not be reported as perfect textual control compliance. Both characters retain many small hair strands and textured shadow marks; a shared neutral sheet can still pull different styles toward the same illustrated face treatment.

The creature is clearly a calm turtle under a translucent lavender umbrella dome, with a low hard shell and broad trailing jelly-like limbs. The two depictions read as views of one design, not two required creatures in a final scene. The main three-quarter view exposes **three distinguishable broad limb masses**; I cannot independently trace a fourth attachment through the overlaps. The small side view also does not resolve four separate appendages. This does not prove the design has only three, but it does mean the setup cannot establish the frozen exact-four count from visible evidence alone.

The creature has much less coral/ornament than the original, yet still has patchy light marks across its head, dome and limb surfaces, plus thin subdivisions within the dome. These remain potential texture-noise sources for downstream transfer. Pony scale is not testable on this isolated sheet without a common scale cue. No obvious generated lettering or signature is visible in either setup.
