# Explore the parts of your world

Open [the component reader](../../docs/world-components/index.html), or double-click [OPEN_GALLERY.cmd](../../docs/world-components/OPEN_GALLERY.cmd) on Windows. To share, copy [the portable ZIP](asset-bundle/local/final-v1/world-components-portable.zip), extract it, and open its `START_HERE.cmd`. It has all nine original favorite drawing directions, each with two new character alternatives, two places, one creature and a three-item gear card. The original reference board is also available for each style.

Use **By style kit** to see how a world’s parts relate. Use **By component** to compare characters, places, creatures or gear across all nine treatments. Keep, reject or leave each card unsure independently; comfort and notes are separate. Add any combination to the mix tray, then export your choices to share or keep a portable copy. Merely viewing a card makes no selection.

The names, jobs and world rules are suggestions. Choosing a face does not commit you to its occupation, choosing a drawing style does not commit you to its world, and the two people in a kit are alternatives rather than a predetermined cast. The earlier comparison actors, turtle and canal are not carried forward as defaults.

A useful next selection is a small collection of parts that intrigue you, with a note about what you like: face, silhouette, atmosphere, creature behavior, tool or story possibility. Once you have those parts, a shared scene can test whether the chosen combination feels like one story. Mixed components may need a common drawing treatment and adjusted materials; the tray itself is a collection of possibilities.

The reader’s optional AI observations describe visible technical limitations; your taste and comfort choices stay yours. See [results](RESULTS.md) for the final evidence and remaining issues, [exact prompts](../../production/world-components/prompts/) for generation instructions, and [the portable bundle guide](asset-bundle/README.md) to share the study.

Native art was generated with the built-in image tool. Every original attempt is preserved, including any repairs; prompt and reference hashes are recorded with each call. Backend model snapshot and seed are not supplied, so the records support reuse of the recipe and references without promising byte-identical regeneration.
