Each new card will receive concise evidence-based findings, separating visible success, visible failure and unresolved/occluded relationships. No numeric quality league table.

- Style fidelity: compare edges, mark scale, shadow construction, proportions and material rendering against the actual original source. A changed palette or subject alone proves neither fidelity nor failure. A failed transfer does not reject the original style.
- Character distinction: C1 and C2 need different facial structure, body mass/proportion, silhouette and posture, with visibly different personality cues. Check differences across all eighteen characters too. Hair, skin color, clothing and accessories alone are insufficient evidence of distinct identity. Adult age and mature acting should read where the brief specifies adults; heavy builds should not automatically become a tiny waist with muscular arms. Check full required framing, limbs, hands and actual prop contact.
- Functional gear: identify each discrete item, its grip/wear interface, load/support path and plausible fictional purpose from the drawing. A useful affordance matters more than ornament. Flag fused parts, impossible finger contact, missing attachment or more than three separate items. Do not demand engineering realism from a clearly fictional mechanism beyond the frozen brief.
- Creature legibility: check complete required silhouette, head/front/back orientation, appendage attachment/count where specified, scale, movement/support and a distinguishable behavioral cue. Novel combinations need readable anatomy, not only decorative surface detail. Cropped or overlapping limbs remain uncertain, not automatically correct.
- Worlds: E1/E2 should differ in spatial layout and lived function, not only weather or palette. Check a readable focal place, depth/scale cues, usable paths/platforms and visual hierarchy. Detail can support a world without filling every region; identify actual crowding rather than equating detail with bad art.
- Cohesion: look for an understandable shared material, shape and functional logic across a direction's people, places, creature and gear. Repeating one ornament everywhere is not enough. This explores compatible components and possible cross-style mixes; it does not establish a plot, final cast or canon.
- Native and phone evidence: inspect every actual source and its real reader rendering, confirming no viewer crop or stale source. Separate face readability from hand/mechanism readability and complete-object readability. At phone width, record value merges, lost appendages, small faces and dense texture that becomes noise. Comfort remains a potential risk assessment until an owner actually reports it; do not manufacture human comprehension or sustained comfort results.

Prioritize a retry only for a concrete frozen requirement failure: missing/cropped essential content, malformed anatomy or contact, extra/fused gear, unreadable creature structure, severe style collapse or unwanted text/signature contamination. Preserve every primary and inspect the retry beside it for new defects. State what a selected repair actually fixes; selection is not blanket acceptance. Mild taste differences, prettier lighting or a more flattering face do not justify a retry.

Watchlist across the set: the same attractive narrow face in every style; C pairs with identical confident half-smiles and heroic stances; all worlds becoming monumental arches or the last canal; all monsters becoming large pets beside the same figure; every tool becoming a decorated weapon; busy source compositions copying themselves into cards intended to isolate components. Genuine diversity can include restraint, age, softness, awkwardness and distinct work roles without exaggerating every feature.
