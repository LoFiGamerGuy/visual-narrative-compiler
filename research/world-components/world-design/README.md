# Nine world-component drafts

`prompt-draft-v2.json` is the prompt conversion input: nine styles, each with C1/C2 adult character candidates, E1 everyday setting, E2 dangerous setting, M1 creature and G1 three-item equipment card. `kits-draft-v2.json` retains the more structured identities, material rules, functions, anatomy and motives. These are exploration hypotheses, not an approved cast or compulsory pairings. The lead owns final prompt freezing and image generation.

The nine worlds are Charge Bridges, Wind Commons, Compost Line, Rain Archive, Downhill Guild, Bell Baths, Moving Steps, Weather Orchard and Last Dye House. Each offers an institutional or ecological cause connecting its components, while its crossmix note gives a way to borrow that cause without adopting the entire kit. Conflicts are optional hooks, not a frozen story.

All eighteen adults have distinct age, face, body, hair and costume briefs. At least one per kit has a clear action or rival stance and a tactical use for existing functional gear. A character's `held_gear` identifies one of the three objects in that same kit's G1 card; it does not request additional equipment. Locations have three principal masses and a readable route. Creature topology is explicit; zero appendages means none, and surface markings are distinguished from limbs.

V1 is preserved as an earlier draft. V2 replaces01's manta-like proposal with a four-legged charge gecko, makes13's spring ram four-legged, strengthens action identities without adding ornament, and records actual rendering distinctions. The02 and05 reference PNGs were directly viewed: they support warm outlined painting and textured painting, respectively, rather than their original intent labels. Lead-provided refinements specify limited soft modeling01, fine directional hatching13 and dense curved copperplate hatching15.

`draft-checks.json` binds both current JSON sources and checks54 category entries,18 distinct adults,27 equipment objects and character-to-card references. It checks authored data, not actual pixels. Later native review must inspect face/silhouette differentiation, exact topology, clear gripping and functional geometry, location routes, material coherence, unnecessary ornament and whether any claimed tool function is actually visible. A pretty image or a passing count alone is not component acceptance.
